# Global workspace theory

Global workspace theory says that consciousness is a brain-wide way of sharing information. Most of what the brain does runs in parallel in many specialized networks, for vision, language, memory, movement and value, and each of these runs without awareness ([specialized processors](record:8bf9461d-fd13-4a3b-af4c-83e071ab474c)). At any moment one coherent content wins a limited-capacity stage, the [global workspace](record:8d5e22ee-8804-4369-bc5f-af2fd4805540), and is broadcast from there to all the other specialists at once ([broadcast](record:f6f764fe-3c28-46b7-9be5-8fc2b364733e)). Bernard Baars proposed this in the 1980s, drawing on the "blackboard" architectures of artificial intelligence, and argued that what we call consciousness is this broadcast: it lets otherwise separate functions work together on novel problems, learning and voluntary control ([the conscious access hypothesis](record:bb51137d-5e08-4865-ba5c-243b8b5f2bcd)).

Stanislas Dehaene, Jean-Pierre Changeux and Lionel Naccache turned the idea into a neural model, the global neuronal workspace. The workspace is made of [neurons with long-range axons](record:2a03cdaa-1e37-4884-b586-1d26f161fb4e), dense in prefrontal, parietal and cingulate cortex. A stimulus becomes conscious when attention amplifies its representation into a brain-scale state of coherent activity ([amplification](record:c9dde85b-c378-4106-97e2-729ac044e37d)). This happens through [ignition](record:f0aa2d47-0957-463c-829c-3641725cc9df): a sudden, nonlinear, self-sustaining reverberation that appears late, roughly 300 ms after a stimulus, and only when the subject reports seeing it. The authors hold that this [global availability is what we experience as a conscious state](record:9b2a360b-2d76-4efb-8a4a-603e641ad90a), and that the sense of self comes from [self-representing modules entering the workspace](record:31adfd89-c6d9-4dbe-8527-104109db48f8).

The theory is tested by contrast: present the same stimulus seen and unseen, by masking, attentional blink or inattention, and look for what differs ([minimal contrast](record:7ca04adb-3d95-42a3-9138-3ed6fc9b33a9)). The differences it predicts are late amplification of sensory activity, long-distance synchrony and prefronto-parietal ignition ([signatures](record:71b4b41d-876a-468e-bd04-70b823272d0f)). Dehaene and Changeux named what would refute it: ignition that turns out unnecessary, artifactual or causally unrelated to conscious states ([refutation](record:dfe9dd7c-e6fa-4ac8-b3db-98ccc777bd3e)).

In 2025 the Cogitate consortium published an adversarial test of the theory against integrated information theory, with predictions preregistered with Dehaene. Category information was found in prefrontal cortex, as predicted, but the predicted [ignition at stimulus offset](record:728e7da7-fe62-45d5-91fc-db0c46ed177f) did not appear, and the predicted [prefrontal synchrony](record:38ac0b81-cf24-485a-92cc-1579c707c79d) failed its preregistered test. Critics also hold that global access does not explain why access is experienced ([the hard problem](record:57d4afb8-0a54-4689-b77d-e55a35c75c35)) and that experience overflows access ([overflow](record:28e89614-15d7-4d51-af0c-9457c26dd491)).
