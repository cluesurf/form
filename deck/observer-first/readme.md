# Algorithmic Idealism

**Community-written from public sources, AI-extracted (claude-opus-5-5), unreviewed. Not written or endorsed by Markus P. Müller.**
To propose a correction, open an issue or a pull request that changes the record in question, citing the source and locator that shows the error. A correction from Markus P. Müller is especially welcome.

Markus P. Müller's algorithmic idealism, first set out as "Law without law" (Quantum, 2020): the first-person question of what an observer will see next is taken as fundamental, its answer is fixed by universal induction on the observer's present state, and the external physical world and an objective reality shared by observers are derived as approximate, emergent descriptions. The folder also records two neighboring results of his: the derivation of the quantum measurement postulates, with Adrian Kent's published objection, and a structural limit on consciousness-based collapse models.

This is a form.surf model repository (format `form.surf/1`). It holds the framework's own claims, each as one JSON record in `proposition/`, and the relations between them in `link/`. Every record names its sources in `provenance.sources`, with a locator and, where it could be checked, a short verbatim quote. `paper/account.md` is the plain account.

## Labels

- `provenance.ai_written: true`: a model extracted the record from the cited source.
- `provenance.reviewed: false`: no person has yet read the record against its source.
- `epistemic_status: "inferred"` marks a result the papers derive (a theorem of the bit model). `hypothesized` marks a postulate, a conjecture or a consequence argued informally.
- `voice: "critique"`: Adrian Kent's published objection to the measurement-postulate derivation, joined by `contradicts` to the claim it targets.
- There are no `observation/` records: the framework's distinctive predictions concern private first-person experiments, and no source reports a test of one.
