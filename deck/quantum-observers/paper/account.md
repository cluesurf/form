Chris Fields starts from quantum information and asks what an observer embedded in the world can actually learn. Take any finite isolated system that evolves [unitarily, which means it conserves observable information](record:2b108d65-fa6b-4065-9068-5b20967e781d), and split it into two parts that interact only weakly. Their interaction is then an exchange of a finite number of bits, [physical interaction as thermodynamics times yes-or-no questions](record:d0283f66-c170-4d19-94e5-57ef892211b4), written on the boundary between them. That boundary is a [holographic screen](record:7102c6c3-3527-4ccd-b662-81bb2e97e4ac), and the [generalized holographic principle](record:eee10d58-34b3-40ea-810f-469501a94105) says that any classical information two finite systems exchange is encoded there. In the language of Karl Friston's free energy principle, [the screen is the Markov blanket](record:fe90eae2-c7a5-4609-ae58-eca401d78290) that separates a system from its world.

An [observer](record:1401610b-d56a-45f7-be2b-77a7659779ad) is any system that reads part of its boundary through [quantum reference frames](record:da201905-dbc5-4699-93cf-1275ede34fef), the physical things that give a measurement its meaning, the way a meter stick does, and keeps a memory. Reading only part of the boundary breaks a symmetry and creates a record, so [decoherence is always relative to an observer](record:6357ea11-5b6b-4342-a1d1-503e430ac9cf). Keeping memory costs free energy, so every observer [trades seeing more of its world against coarse-graining what it keeps](record:d6217e4a-08a1-4f1e-bba2-6924670b85a6). Which reference frame a system implements is [undecidable from outside](record:c6373cd7-da6f-4453-a787-bfdc366c851e), and Fields holds that there is [no preferred way to divide the universe into systems](record:b0a2543b-7fc2-4e58-96e2-c948d8893284) at all. From this he argues that quantum Darwinism [has to assume the redundancy it is meant to explain](record:28aa165a-656d-4d8d-95f4-c697fe3d8afd).

With Friston, Glazebrook and Levin, Fields shows that [the free energy principle is, asymptotically, the principle of unitarity](record:2eee589b-a474-45f1-8e69-c817669ccbd3), so [agency is the generic case](record:1d3b823b-fb72-479c-889a-aef302f93d76) and the merely physical system is the exception. With Levin he extends this to biology: [cognition is scale-free](record:4f941517-e7bd-49d5-bc3b-ee40a60511d3), cells may [use quantum coherence to compute](record:224433cd-8238-413f-8ece-673f8b5669b4), and [recently separated sister cells should violate Bell inequalities](record:64c8d988-c4af-4560-8e2b-6b650e31f795). With Glazebrook and Marcianò he argues that [the codes agents build when they communicate induce spacetime on their boundaries](record:32cf0642-658a-40ab-b2a9-73a0f0d37f38).

These biological predictions are untested. Critics argue that [decoherence makes the brain classical](record:690fedee-89d2-4e2a-bf63-f5794e39c7fd) and that [the free energy principle holds only for a narrow class of systems](record:1abbde12-3116-4fbb-97d3-8d977e7f3e44), and quantum Darwinism's proponents hold that [classical objectivity can be derived inside quantum theory](record:a5d41ef8-a1a3-4bc8-9ba2-e7f4ce3d34ad).
