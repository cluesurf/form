Andrei Khrennikov reads quantum theory as a tool for reasoning with missing information. On his reading, the formalism of state vectors, amplitudes and noncommuting observables is [a general calculus for consistent processing of incomplete information about contexts](record:12c4b9f0-ee4c-45ef-996f-daf8c9a1fbde), and quantum randomness is [the trace of that incompleteness rather than irreducible chance](record:6ba509fb-bd4b-4489-b7ba-e5962ebcdc78). A context, the complex of mental, social or economic conditions a person is in, is [represented by a complex probability amplitude](record:d7b3a15b-a5f6-4f1e-b1e4-10066ad0aace). Nothing in this requires a small or exotic system, so a macroscopic brain can be [a quantum-like computer](record:088717cd-dd6e-477f-a554-ec67aa630311) whose physical basis is ordinary neural networks. He separates this sharply from [theories of genuine quantum physical processes in the brain, such as Penrose and Hameroff's](record:d2c6bcd3-4529-4084-81f2-8dcf1b6b0021), and holds that the model stands [whoever wins the debate over microtubule coherence](record:6ba528e3-a69e-4ade-a1a5-a61f682aea50).

The empirical side is human judgment. Answers to questions show [interference of probabilities](record:a17c075b-be9a-4915-b266-e0856f79049f), order effects, and context dependence. Asking a question is a measurement with back action, and the class of measurements matters: projective measurements, the textbook kind, [fail on data from aesthetic perception](record:a510bd19-f82f-4ffd-84b9-0a1b2a5053cc), so Khrennikov, Fuyama and Ozawa argue that advanced cognition uses [sharp repeatable non-projective measurements](record:002fe278-eaf1-430e-9d19-27c042584769), a class physics barely uses, and that [physics and cognition explore different parts of measurement theory](record:a2726c53-2821-4761-9f6a-d32965c2028b). With Dzhafarov's Contextuality-by-Default, the program found that [earlier claims of contextuality in cognition were direct influences](record:7af17370-240d-462a-ad5a-836747b28052), and then that [crowdsourcing experiments do show true contextuality](record:38480af3-38b0-48d0-8c0a-f91374ae3e3e). A model of consciousness follows the same lines: the brain [measures itself](record:47aa29c7-78ea-448a-a648-8ba40307cfc5), with unconsciousness as the system and consciousness as the observer, and emotions [set the context in which perceptions are read out](record:d10f05cf-8ce2-4515-aae4-bca5cdada9ad).

A second program replaces the real line as the model of mind. Since mind is [not infinitely divisible and its states are hierarchical](record:1aee006e-a00f-489c-844f-edd1a50f6830), mental space is [an m-adic tree with an ultrametric](record:e86b14f6-3668-4d03-abc7-0cab6e21e62f), where a branch is a mental image, a ball is an association and a collection of balls is an idea, and [conscious and unconscious flows are dynamics on that tree](record:428a97a7-dc90-47cd-8e66-7300f7c77459). Khrennikov expects an adequate model of mental space [to take centuries](record:8bfa122f-6521-4960-a5f3-4218fea1e11d). With Oded Shor and Felix Benninger he extends the tree to the universe: Dendrogramic Holographic Theory treats the universe as [made solely of events arranged in a p-adic tree](record:9f7ae07c-a751-4b65-8bf9-d8f5e9026437), grounded in [Leibniz's principle of indiscernibles](record:5465417f-88ae-46b1-bcec-e71bf2761b1f), from which [Bohmian mechanics follows](record:63dbcdb5-4084-45f3-8d16-7c28f23c323f) and [space-time is derived, relative to an observer](record:d0471f9c-8490-4c2a-8f87-c9b27818bf14). Khrennikov states that [present experiments cannot yet support or reject the quantum-like model](record:b800aaaa-d672-4849-95ac-5240c835b171) and that [no accepted model says how the brain forms superpositions of mental states](record:56f7574c-7ef2-40e9-9f60-fcc30476fd5e).
