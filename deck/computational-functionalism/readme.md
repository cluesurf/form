# Computational Functionalism

Community-written from public sources, AI-extracted, unreviewed. Not written or endorsed by Hilary Putnam, Patrick Butlin, Robert Long and David J. Chalmers. To propose a correction, open an issue or pull request that changes the record and cites the source.

This is a [form.surf](https://form.surf) model repository for computational functionalism about consciousness: the thesis that what makes a state a mental state, and a conscious one, is the role it plays in a system's computation, not what the system is made of. It starts from Hilary Putnam's machine state functionalism of 1967, stated here from the Stanford Encyclopedia entry because the 1967 paper has no open copy, and the Churchlands' 1981 defense of functionalism against the qualia objections. Its present form is the 2023 report Consciousness in Artificial Intelligence by Patrick Butlin, Robert Long and seventeen coauthors, which adopts computational functionalism as a working hypothesis and derives indicator properties from scientific theories of consciousness, and David J. Chalmers's 2023 lecture on whether a large language model could be conscious. Each claim is one JSON record in `proposition/`, with its kind, its epistemic status, and the source and page it was taken from. The working hypothesis is marked hypothesized, the indicator method designed, and the assessments of current systems inferred. The Chinese room, absent and inverted qualia, and the view that consciousness needs life or a specific material are kept as claims with `voice: "critique"`, beside the replies the sources give.

Related folders: [biological-naturalism](../biological-naturalism/) holds Searle's own view, that consciousness needs the causal powers of brains. The theories the indicator properties are drawn from have their own folders: [recurrent-processing-theory](../recurrent-processing-theory/), [global-workspace](../global-workspace/), [higher-order-theories](../higher-order-theories/), [attention-schema-theory](../attention-schema-theory/) and [predictive-processing](../predictive-processing/). [iit](../iit/) is the theory the report leaves out because it is not computational. [eliminative-materialism](../eliminative-materialism/) holds the Churchlands' later neurocomputational view.

Every record has `provenance.ai_written: true` and `reviewed: false` until a named person checks it against its sources.

Format: form.surf/1. Check with `form check .`.
