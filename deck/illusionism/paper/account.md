# Illusionism

Most theories of consciousness accept that experiences have [phenomenal properties](record:30e6e5be-6c66-490a-9fea-3d09f35dd905), qualitative feels, and try to say how they arise. Frankish sorts them into radical realists, who think explaining them needs new science, and conservative realists, who think current science will do. [Illusionism](record:c3f3dec8-7973-40fb-b14a-701902f5e93b) is a third option: [phenomenal consciousness is an illusion](record:5eb9d0b6-5fe3-4d17-a47a-b14d497871df), and the task is [the illusion problem](record:ee6773c2-ec65-44b6-9911-e1c4ca7cb650), explaining why experiences seem to have phenomenal properties.

The view is strong illusionism. [Weak illusionism](record:38ef1349-47a3-4598-91d2-bf82a395c65b) keeps real phenomenal properties and denies only that they are ineffable, intrinsic or private. Strong illusionism says that what introspection tracks are [quasi-phenomenal properties](record:a1b7af33-0806-4666-ab97-40cd96d1c117), physical properties that introspection [misrepresents as phenomenal](record:af6b4826-1c3c-4063-9d43-37fe2abd8c2b), [bundling their complex detail into a simple feel](record:7cb2a504-f307-4d26-839a-4057dccecef6). Frankish argues that weak illusionism [collapses into the strong form](record:8cf3914e-10cd-4837-a1e2-cfa2732ab1e8) unless it can say what makes a property phenomenal beyond this. [Consciousness in an inclusive sense still exists](record:a541d56a-45a6-49ea-9520-534c06efd2e3), and [experiences are like something](record:7cd5426e-6fcf-4800-8efc-5d2e01173e4c) in the functional sense of being introspectively represented.

The case for the view is conservative and abductive. Radical realism [faces epiphenomenalism](record:89565fa5-cf4d-47e1-b846-05f3bf3ed375), conservative realism is [unstable](record:994db9c6-5800-475c-9e9b-4ec9d80c2ba9), and our beliefs about consciousness [could be explained without phenomenal properties](record:9f452cb8-35e6-4b0c-b203-438857774a8d), while [apparent anomalousness is itself evidence of illusion](record:f284b5bc-b148-47d3-b1f2-b0d7cbf929fe). Phenomenal properties survive as [intentional objects](record:844426bf-3a77-41ba-a783-34acd993130b), and the illusion may [have a function](record:ef3c4128-5423-485a-b740-4c0f57a8f69a).

Dennett's version reaches the same place by several routes. [Quining Qualia](record:e7b3d2b7-7a2f-4579-af45-7df207492c21) argues that [qualia as ineffable, intrinsic, private and directly apprehensible](record:c6a649e8-d816-41a7-8d2e-f0160b53382f) do not exist, and that what is real are [practically ineffable properties tracked by idiosyncratic detectors](record:5284ff1e-5930-403b-9f53-484261e6b721). The [multiple drafts model](record:d3238c74-3300-4fb4-8a35-81ecb7c16185) replaces the [Cartesian theater](record:654885f8-48dc-4478-b8db-c1cc5cf703ff) with distributed, continually revised content, so that [subjective order is an interpretation](record:37167c2b-e8fa-4557-8850-f7a3bd50dd7f). [Heterophenomenology](record:a8038f5d-325a-4fd5-aa58-a1b310a12e02) takes subjects' beliefs about their experience as the data. Dennett holds illusionism to be [the default theory](record:f211964e-b084-4efa-adb9-23cc2581729f), asks of every theory [the hard question, and then what happens](record:27a65bd0-bba3-4c32-aaaf-e66396055c4f), and calls himself [a strong illusionist](record:9861ecc5-c1ec-42a4-a5d5-91d75f82603e).

Critics answer that phenomenal consciousness can be [defined innocently by examples](record:ba0c965c-c09b-41db-932c-52798d34894b), that strong illusionism [denies that people feel pain](record:e3eee960-3856-4824-a7cd-bba2bd44fca9), and that the illusionist still owes [an account of how a mind without phenomenal consciousness could seem like this](record:ed7751a2-bf66-4702-afbe-9889028cb100).
