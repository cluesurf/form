# The account

This repository states, from public sources, the program of the Qualia Research Institute (QRI), chiefly Andrés Gómez-Emilsson and Michael Edward Johnson, with Chris Percy on the recent papers. It is community-written and AI-extracted, not written or endorsed by them.

QRI starts from one bet, [qualia formalism](record:87fa58ba-a6c1-4a5d-b47a-ba298a93a00d): for every conscious experience there is, in principle, a mathematical object with exactly the same structure. If that holds, experience can be measured like charge or mass. QRI adds that [valence](record:c3f5cfc7-0cb5-48b3-829e-d04b3e62f7e8), how good or bad an experience feels, is a [crisp, measurable quantity](record:757ea101-bdf4-4c03-af3b-0a892c43a221), and proposes what it is. The [symmetry theory of valence](record:c82c7c92-5d96-4c1d-96a1-9d1c73416e52) says that the pleasantness of an experience is the symmetry of its mathematical object. Harmony feels good [because it is symmetry over time](record:b50f0b4f-031f-4640-8bad-8d1dec3d6e5a), and dissonance in the brain [is suffering](record:dabb92c1-31d7-479a-a95b-23924cffa57c).

The theory is the testable center of the program, so QRI names proxies a lab could measure. Symmetric structures [compress better](record:d603b365-6539-4a1c-bb01-ef762dab2cde), so [pleasant brain states should give more compressible EEG](record:ccf53c8d-7e12-4058-a82a-eee8b88b5640). The brain's activity can be decomposed into [connectome-specific harmonic waves](record:7e374156-fd63-451d-9215-c2d648dae420), and [consonance among those harmonics](record:b23f0989-ee95-4315-80c5-c9935636b45d) should track valence. [Neural annealing](record:deb700c1-2f22-450e-97d7-aec45d755f57) describes how the brain heats into high-energy states and settles into new, more harmonious patterns, and links the account to meditation, psychedelics and trauma. On psychedelics, Gómez-Emilsson argues that [DMT bends phenomenal space into hyperbolic geometry](record:a45d95c5-17cb-470a-9c48-163ccf4492d5). With Percy he argues that pleasure and pain are [heavy-tailed](record:18e77377-2c12-427e-9ff2-70cd2c328f05), spanning at least two orders of magnitude.

The second strand asks what makes one experience one. The [boundary problem](record:af8b86f1-9926-4983-8e87-1d7c75503036) asks why a unified point of view has hard edges. QRI's answer is that [a point of view is a topologically bounded pocket of a physical field](record:7c8b99dc-d973-4835-900d-340b33ae0a12), most likely the brain's electromagnetic field. Topology is [the same in every reference frame](record:5680717a-66e7-4d47-9197-53d63f145c14), so these boundaries are objective, and [they have effects](record:2d7533b5-920a-4cf8-aa50-75c5f1605069) that evolution could select. The authors call the solution [conditional](record:b4c4967b-b91b-4794-83bd-fee61601b7c8) and name a test: matching topologies should appear [at a consistent spatial scale](record:d04f9ed4-1519-49e3-87cc-d414f5c5c792). Critics reply that [no empirical research supports the valence theory](record:59e85f7b-574d-4c59-ac44-ead959344726), that [valence is more likely a computational signal](record:2a7c37e3-ef90-41e8-a6ae-5333c151f156), and that the field account's [locality requirement is ad hoc](record:744a37e2-cad8-4447-811b-26f5c3a29387).
