# Russellian Monism

Russellian monism starts from a claim about what physics says. On [structuralism about physics](record:c454005f-2909-4026-8733-b59a4cfe06f4), physics describes the world by its spatiotemporal structure and dynamics, what mass and charge do, and is silent on what does it. Russell put it in The Analysis of Matter: [percepts are the only part of the physical world known otherwise than abstractly](record:3b5ff816-62bc-4465-bb77-c63025ec1afc), and [nothing in physics shows the physical world to differ in character from the mental](record:6f879e60-2dbe-4bce-896f-0873fb0b3aa0). Since [percepts are in the brain](record:f8090003-6976-457b-8f15-8340f720734c), the matter of a brain includes experience. Eddington, a year later, called the atom of physics [a schedule of pointer readings](record:22edb6a9-23d0-427f-87ee-9bcb20d42e28) attached to an unknown background, and proposed that [the background is of a spiritual nature](record:fda89a86-a231-4b3e-9550-fcbef6981e6c), since for the readings of his own brain he has an insight beyond them. His conclusion, put crudely, was that [the stuff of the world is mind-stuff](record:58b9c572-07ee-4866-ab72-e58311535f97).

The modern view joins three theses. Beside structuralism there is [realism about quiddities](record:cb9d368d-c0b5-4324-85a2-805ab816949b), the claim that intrinsic or categorical properties ground the dispositions physics describes, and [quidditism about consciousness](record:edf992c0-7c75-4ecc-94a5-ff484e75d3cb), the claim that those same properties constitute consciousness at least in part. Chalmers named one form [type-F monism](record:c42ea504-0f47-4474-9a3a-71c7750a2cbc) and argued that it [solves two problems at once](record:23299e70-ac5e-422e-86fc-bf08c55086d3), giving matter an intrinsic nature and giving consciousness a place in nature. It is [compatible with the causal closure of the microphysical](record:5ab10143-1d7c-45eb-86af-7d9ec6485a32), which dualism is not, and it [answers the conceivability and knowledge arguments](record:decd12fa-e516-4a3e-8088-781723efa440), which traditional physicalism cannot, so it is offered as [a middle way between physicalism and dualism](record:6380ec7c-1980-48fc-9124-b53118c23a17).

The view comes in kinds. On [Russellian panpsychism](record:e5398fa8-06e1-4dfe-8b33-b21a9462902a) the quiddities are themselves phenomenal. On [Russellian panprotopsychism](record:33de90f8-be7d-40fc-ba97-1f7f97e40d12) they are [protophenomenal](record:ddf8f6f2-942a-4a3e-97d4-71ae5010e887), not experiences but able to constitute them. Pereboom's [physicalist Russellian monism](record:d3d47521-4500-4d28-9fe9-1eab7e730f8b) takes them to be [absolutely intrinsic properties](record:4d55e625-ab94-4337-976d-379cf22d4084), close enough to paradigm physical properties to count as physical, and holds that [protophenomenalism is the physicalist's best hope](record:4c2fb1bb-a368-446f-b1a7-21de6eaa5189). [Constitutive and emergentist forms](record:aa469e34-6cbe-4020-b7a8-77380ea2a179) differ on how macro experience relates to the quiddities, and Goff defends [intelligible emergentist Russellian monism](record:a0a6dfdd-04ff-4b57-b0e4-2f698c49236a). Most defenders agree that [little is known of the quiddities](record:4f7c5b82-9063-493b-b2a8-658748ac4719) beyond their roles.

The texts press several objections. The [combination problem](record:45d1e61b-0800-41c7-80d1-c24f1f39fb7e), which Chalmers calls the most serious, asks how micro quiddities add up to unified experience, and the [subject-summing problem](record:6c350cd3-2a15-420e-85b2-67fad55f0af4) asks how subjects compose a subject. Goff argues that [constitutive forms are implausible](record:48737b63-d25d-44fb-8c7f-dc8b36294528), and others raise a [revenge problem](record:17751015-70a7-4b9d-9318-69e049b4ca01) for panprotopsychism. Howell argues that [phenomenality is along for the causal ride](record:b735e233-b21a-4bca-8a48-f443c90f7c04), which Alter and Coleman answer by [separating metaphysical from nomological contingency](record:89bf9ca2-b457-41a8-aa0d-69e097df945e). Kind argues the view is [unmotivated](record:8044e1da-cbfa-4434-a705-72acab0ec6ae), Stoljar and others question [the structural and nonstructural distinction](record:fa23513f-ed21-41ab-a888-29923ca60981), and Newman's problem holds that [a purely structural description is near vacuous](record:2f6ea150-333b-43af-bcdd-38006ee437e6).
