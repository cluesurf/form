# Analytic idealism

Analytic idealism is Bernardo Kastrup's claim that [there is only one consciousness](record:54eac2d8-d0ed-4638-9caf-aa7aeffa2ed1), and that everything else is a pattern within it. He starts from the one thing he takes as given, experience, and declines to posit anything non-experiential beneath it. Physicalism, he argues, cannot get the qualities of experience from the relational parameters of matter ([the hard problem](record:04d4f968-e9c7-4122-bf33-a28003618459)), and bottom-up panpsychism cannot explain how tiny subjects merge into one mind. Idealism avoids both by starting from one experiential field, and it is [the more parsimonious inference](record:70b13fe7-b5fc-4cbc-883a-db4c8cb9b09d): it extends consciousness beyond the personal, instead of positing a second kind of thing. Particular experiences are [self-excitations of that field](record:1f3a21e1-ae8b-4d98-a885-b71e088fca31), as a dance is the dancer in motion.

The hard question for this view is the reverse of panpsychism's: how does one mind become many? Kastrup's answer is [dissociation](record:68d7b0a8-99c7-429b-a860-1bcebfceac95), the clinical process seen in dissociative identity disorder, where one psyche forms several co-conscious alters, each blind to the others. [We are dissociated alters](record:71a1de21-ac36-4b06-abce-725303b43789) of cosmic consciousness. Dissociation happens [at the level of living organisms](record:6fca6004-f08b-4496-a469-513fe7c2d931), and a [living body is what an alter looks like from outside](record:9deb305f-4536-4346-b8a7-5feacbe6f034). The inanimate world is [the outside appearance of cosmic thoughts](record:6774690b-54ec-47ff-8705-ca42fe925636) that surround us, and [perception is those thoughts impinging on our boundary](record:0beebdb4-9f16-414e-9bd1-e67f6671faa4), coded by evolution for survival rather than truth.

This explains why brain activity tracks experience: the [brain is the image of experience, not its cause](record:2f09a45d-387e-4d10-8c07-ae48571b6236). Kastrup points to empirical bridges: a patient whose [blind alters lacked visual brain responses](record:9d59395c-3a35-4551-ab82-990b283eb97b), brain scans that tell [real dissociation from acted dissociation](record:e931eed1-4c79-4f55-97e6-0da38cbc8371), and a [pattern of impaired brain function accompanying richer experience](record:1c0249f5-1fdd-49e9-a382-d2b568710cc5), including psychedelic states. On this view [death ends a dissociation](record:c1339cc7-273d-4e4a-b714-8f640371d6f7), not consciousness, and building a conscious machine would require [creating life](record:5fa1fe00-d8af-49e8-9035-153850dc0420).

Critics reply that the universe shows nothing like the cognitive fragmentation of a DID patient ([disanalogy](record:8365630c-1c16-43aa-ac6a-d5a87ea84c42)), that the view makes us unaware of most of our own experience ([introspection](record:87268117-a7f5-4ddc-a23b-2455ba7b28bd)), that quantum results do not favor it over realism ([quantum](record:afecc06f-3dca-44cc-8456-3e7cdfc9c7a4)), and that psychedelic brains show higher signal complexity, not simply less activity ([signal diversity](record:12b17b28-1233-4154-a60a-b71f76924703)).
