# Epiphenomenalism

Community-written from public sources, AI-extracted, unreviewed. Not written or endorsed by Thomas H. Huxley and Frank Jackson. To propose a correction, open an issue or pull request that changes the record and cites the source.

This is a [form.surf](https://form.surf) model repository for epiphenomenalism: the view that mental events are caused by physical events in the brain but have no effects on any physical event. Thomas H. Huxley stated it in 1874 as conscious automatism, comparing consciousness to the steam whistle of a locomotive. Frank Jackson revived it in 1982 for qualia, arguing from the knowledge argument (Mary, who knows all the physical information about color vision, still learns something when she first sees red) that qualia are nonphysical, and from the causal completeness of physics that they do nothing. Jackson later gave the view up, and that change is recorded here as his own claim. Jackson's 1982 and 1986 papers have no open copy, so his words are quoted from the Stanford Encyclopedia entries that print them. Each claim is one JSON record in `proposition/`, with its kind, its epistemic status, and the source and section it was taken from. The objections from natural selection, other minds and self-stultification, with the replies the epiphenomenalist literature gives, are kept as claims with `voice: "critique"` and their replies.

Related folders: [interactionist-dualism](../interactionist-dualism/) holds the dualism that keeps mental causation, and Popper and Eccles's use of the natural selection objection against this view. [illusionism](../illusionism/) argues that radical realism about phenomenal consciousness faces exactly this epiphenomenalism. [russellian-monism](../russellian-monism/) is one answer to the worry, recorded here, that intrinsic properties of matter are themselves epiphenomenal. [eliminative-materialism](../eliminative-materialism/) holds Paul Churchland's reply to the knowledge argument.

Every record has `provenance.ai_written: true` and `reviewed: false` until a named person checks it against its sources.

Format: form.surf/1. Check with `form check .`.
