# Epiphenomenalism

[Epiphenomenalism](record:8932d812-581d-4cb6-9104-ec9097cb7d44) is the view that mental events are caused by brain events and cause nothing physical. Its motivation is [causal closure](record:87079e0e-8335-4ecd-a8d7-56fb5b4d934e): wherever a physical event has a sufficient cause, it has a sufficient physical cause, so a nonphysical mental event that made a difference would break physical law, and [adding a mental cause on top](record:2827934c-0523-4918-9eb9-6a08734b8bfe) offends parsimony without giving the mind a difference to make. The argument [assumes dualism](record:1ca5a1d8-a778-4b31-adda-671755023b56) and is often [a fall-back position](record:7aed0010-7abf-4f60-9c6b-05d202d1220f).

Huxley gave the view its classic image. From frogs that [swim as well without the brain regions of consciousness](record:0171eace-416a-4e14-9454-ec96870a650f), he concluded that animal consciousness is [a collateral product, like a steam whistle](record:f503f5c0-d6ee-4321-b14b-96307566976c), that [the same holds of humans](record:c73e5433-90d9-42c1-a772-b429457c3527), and that [volition is a symbol of the brain state that causes the act](record:10408857-1331-4724-90be-c9f87eae9cf1). Humans are [conscious automata](record:01238cfe-f2c1-4d13-a01b-108af3f3146f).

Jackson's case begins from [the knowledge argument](record:f667ff98-5ea0-42de-9508-f7c04ec6042c): Mary has all the [physical information](record:88f3a6aa-8ccf-4047-9e3b-cc3ea575ea3d) about color vision, yet learns something when she sees color, so [qualia are not physical](record:dfa9ac70-257f-4c58-b568-27fccf771015). With causal closure they are [epiphenomenal](record:7c6bbff8-fca3-44c2-aab3-2107ffe7eea2). Against the objection from evolution he answers that [qualia are a by-product, like the weight of a polar bear's coat](record:34a0fc64-ec51-4a24-9285-f877ba439160). Later [Jackson gave the view up](record:03a4d52d-7627-438c-9ebb-15eb46b1c0e3), holding that epiphenomenal qualia could not be known.

The objections are that the view is [obviously absurd](record:0d5b8b75-6e9c-4c5a-b9ec-c6269ec5c9b3), that [consciousness could not have evolved](record:893de7aa-72cf-43d2-b86f-78148740197f), that the [fit of pleasure with benefit](record:20e239dc-2436-4a64-a117-9b5ba1826752) would be a coincidence, that [other minds become unknowable](record:8f4d6579-79fd-47a3-b497-ae00edcba230), and, strongest, that [the view refutes itself](record:eb8eaa5f-3fd8-4f61-94bb-94ce971b5b12). Epiphenomenalists reply that [causation is never obvious](record:bd7ab02a-4f21-4013-a036-11674da6b802), that [the neural causes are what is selected](record:a50455cd-6195-49b7-a705-77a2287d903c), with [a reward system](record:53cd9c82-5c77-41f2-a550-1cf9fcc3a8c7) behind pleasure, that [similar causes support other minds](record:2a2f4f47-3f3a-454b-b1cc-0b9cdce66a10), and that [knowing an experience need not be caused by it](record:a85a3393-cc8e-4897-817f-5d92faa091bd). In the age of materialism, [causal exclusion](record:f5b22db3-f5b5-4141-913d-45934512a7df) and the [unexplained link to qualia](record:e7064c06-4759-4b03-95a3-59fa476850da) keep the question open, and [intrinsic properties may be epiphenomenal too](record:956f1eed-d9f5-4bdc-b960-6a0ab59111af).
